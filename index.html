Each tap on an NXP NTAG 424 DNA chip produces a one-time signed URL (SUN message). A copied tag replays an old counter, so the server rejects it. Popl currently tells Amazon buyers to "avoid fake knockoffs"; this makes knockoffs not work.

Fleet controls

  • Rep leaves the company: card is reassigned or killed from the admin console, same day as the HR offboarding event.
  • Lost card: one-tap revoke, replacement ships with the same profile.
  • Every physical card has a serial, owner and last-tap date, so IT can audit hardware like laptops.

Where Popl's hardware comes from

  • Sales channel: Popl says its NFC accessories (cards, phone tags, wristbands, keychains) are sold only through Amazon.
  • Manufacturer: not disclosed. Amazon lists "Popl" as the manufacturer, which is the brand, not the factory. I couldn't find public import records under Popl Co.
  • IP: Popl holds US patents (10949725, 11487982, 12050951) on a removable accessory with an NFC tag on a substrate, inventor Jason Alvarez-Cohen. The patent covers the design, so the factory can be swapped freely.
  • Known partner: MOFT co-built a phone stand with a Popl NFC antenna (vegan leather, magnets, aluminum antenna).
  • Most likely setup: contract manufacturing in the Shenzhen/Guangdong NFC card cluster, using commodity NXP NTAG chips. That's where the vast majority of NFC business cards and tags are made. Treat this as an informed inference, not a confirmed fact.

Why split it this way

  • Buy chips from authorized NXP distributors, not the card factory. Counterfeit NTAGs are common and you can't run 424 DNA security on a fake.
  • Load the encryption keys yourself after import. The factory never holds them, which is a clean SOC 2 story.
  • Keep a second card supplier in Vietnam or Mexico so a tariff change doesn't halt enterprise orders.
  • Sell direct to Teams accounts with fleet pricing instead of Amazon only, so hardware shows up on the same invoice as seats.

Where Tapline beats Popl

Pricing

Popl charges per badge scan on event plans, and G2 reviewers complain about features locked behind paid tiers. Tapline charges flat per event, unlimited scans.

Follow-up

Popl syncs the lead in about 9 seconds, then the rep is on their own. Tapline scores the lead and drafts the email from the rep's own booth notes.

Attribution

Tapline reads closed-won back from Salesforce or HubSpot, so "200x ROI" claims come from the customer's own CRM, not a case study.

Hardware

Signed taps stop clones, and cards are managed as a fleet tied to HR, the same system Popl already uses for seats.
